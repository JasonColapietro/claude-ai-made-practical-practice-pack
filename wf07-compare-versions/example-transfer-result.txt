PROMPT USED (the chapter prompt with the transfer lines swapped in):

I'm an [OPERATIONS COORDINATOR AT A PHYSICAL THERAPY PRACTICE]. Our PTO policy was rewritten, and the changes are not marked. Compare the two attached files and tell me every difference, so I can explain them to staff before they ask.

Files:
- [wf07-transfer-pto-policy-old.txt] is the current policy (Version 1).
- [wf07-transfer-pto-policy-new.txt] is the new policy (Version 2).
Use only these two files.

Work section by section, from the first section to the last. Compare every number, date, dollar amount, percentage, deadline and name. Also look for sentences that were added or deleted.

Give me three things:
1. A table of every change with these columns: Section | Version 1 says | Version 2 says | What this means for staff. Quote the exact words from each version, kept short. Put the changes staff will notice first.
2. A short list of cosmetic changes that do not change the meaning, such as reworded sentences or renumbered sections.
3. A list of the sections that did not change.

Rules:
- If a section was only moved or renumbered, say so. Do not report it as new or deleted.
- If you are not sure whether a change matters, list it anyway and say why you are unsure.
- Explain in plain English. Do not give legal advice.
- Do not skip small changes. A single changed number counts.

----------------------------------------------------------------
RESULT

I compared the two policies section by section. There are 13 changes that affect staff, plus one wording change that runs through the whole document. The headline for staff: sick time now has its own bank, but less PTO carries over, December time off is limited, and longer trips need more notice.

1. CHANGES THAT AFFECT MEANING (the ones staff will notice first)

| Section | Version 1 says | Version 2 says | What this means for staff |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6.1 | "carry over up to 40 hours of unused PTO" | "carry over up to 24 hours of unused PTO" | Staff can roll over 16 fewer hours into the next year. Anyone used to banking 40 hours will lose the extra 16 on January 1. |
| 1.1 and 5.1 | "PTO covers vacation, personal days and sick days in one bank of hours." | "Sick time is now handled separately (see Section 5)." and "24 hours of paid sick time on January 1 of each year (prorated for team members hired during the year)" | Sick days no longer come out of PTO first. Everyone gets a separate 24 hours (3 eight-hour days) of sick time each January 1. It does not carry over and is not paid out when someone leaves. |
| 5.3 (was 5.2) | "PTO is used for unplanned absences." | "Unplanned absences use sick time first, then PTO." | When someone calls out sick, the 24 sick hours are used before any PTO. Only after those are gone does PTO get used. |
| 4.5 (new) | (No December rule.) | "From December 15 to December 31, no more than two team members per clinic may be on PTO on the same day. Requests for these dates received by October 1 are reviewed together." | Holiday time off is now limited to two people per clinic per day. Staff who want late-December days should request them by October 1. After that, it is first come, first served if spots remain. |
| 4.2 | "at least 2 weeks before the first day off" | "at least 3 weeks before the first day off" | Requests for 3 or more days in a row need one more week of notice. The 48-hour guideline for 1 or 2 days is unchanged. |
| 4.3 | "Requests are approved by the employee's clinic manager." | "Requests for 5 or more consecutive days also need approval from the Operations Coordinator." | Week-long trips need a second approval from you. Unsure point: the policy still says managers respond within 5 business days, but it does not say how long the Operations Coordinator has to respond. |
| 2.2 | "after completing 60 days of employment" | "after completing 90 days of employment" | New hires wait 30 days longer before they can use PTO. They still earn it from day one. Unsure point: the policy does not say whether the 90-day wait also applies to the new sick time. |
| 3.1 | "earn 4.62 hours of PTO each pay period. This equals about 120 hours (15 days) a year." | "(a) Less than 3 years of service: 4.62 hours ... (b) 3 or more years of service: 6.15 hours per pay period, about 160 hours (20 days) a year." | Full-time staff with 3 or more years of service earn 5 more days a year. Staff under 3 years earn the same as before. |
| 3.2 | "in proportion to the hours they work, at the same rate as full-time employees" | "1 hour of PTO for every 30 hours worked" | Part-time staff now have a fixed rate. Unsure point: the old policy never gave a per-hour rate, so this cannot be compared exactly. As an estimate only: if a full-time pay period is 80 hours, 4.62 hours of PTO works out to about 1 hour per 17 hours worked, so 1 per 30 would be less. Check the actual rate payroll used before you tell part-time staff whether they earn more or less. |
| 5.2 (was 5.1) | "at least 1 hour before their shift starts. A text message is not enough." | "must call or text their clinic manager at least 2 hours before their shift starts" | Staff now need to give 2 hours' notice instead of 1, but a text now counts. |
| 8.1 | "are paid for all unused PTO in their final paycheck" | "are paid for unused PTO in their final paycheck, up to a maximum of 40 hours" | Payout when someone resigns with proper notice is now capped at 40 hours. Anything above that is lost. Sick time is never paid out (5.1). |
| Header | "Effective January 1, 2025." | "Effective January 1, 2027." | The new rules start January 1, 2027. Unsure point: the December rule in 4.5 mentions an October 1 request date, but the policy does not say whether that applies to December 2026 requests, which fall under the old policy. |

2. COSMETIC CHANGES (no change in meaning)

- "Employees" and "employee's" became "team members" and "team member's" throughout, including in 2.1, 4.1, 4.4, 7.2, 8.2 and 9.1. Same people, same meaning.
- Section 5's heading changed from "UNPLANNED ABSENCES" to "SICK TIME AND UNPLANNED ABSENCES" to fit the new sick time.
- Old 5.3 (a provider's note after 3 or more consecutive sick days) is now 5.4. The wording is the same.

3. SECTIONS THAT DID NOT CHANGE (apart from "team members")

- 2.1 (who is eligible; per-diem and temporary staff still excluded)
- 3.3 (no accrual on overtime or unpaid leave)
- 4.1 (requests go through the scheduling system)
- 4.4 (same dates approved in the order received)
- 5.4, formerly 5.3 (provider's note after 3 sick days)
- 7.1 and 7.2 (six paid holidays, substitute day within 30 days)
- 8.2 (no payout with short notice or dismissal for cause)
- 9.1 (questions go to the clinic manager or to you)

This is a plain-English comparison of the two documents, not legal or HR compliance advice.
