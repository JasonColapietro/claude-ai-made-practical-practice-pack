2. WHAT MAKES THESE QUOTES HARD TO COMPARE DIRECTLY

- Different estimate types. Buckeye and Two Rivers are binding. Great Plains is binding not-to-exceed (it can go down, not up). Rocky Ridge is non-binding, so $6,280 can rise if the shipment weighs more. Mile High does not say.
- Different weights from different surveys. The weights range from 7,200 lbs (Rocky Ridge, from an online form) to 8,100 lbs (Great Plains, from an in-home visit). Buckeye used a video survey. A low weight on a non-binding estimate can mean a higher final bill.
- Packing is not the same. Great Plains packs everything. Rocky Ridge packs only the kitchen. Buckeye and Two Rivers expect you to pack (Two Rivers sells the materials). Mile High has you pack, load and unload yourselves.
- Protection is not the same. Only Great Plains includes full value protection (up to $50,000). Buckeye adds it for $389 (up to $60,000) and Two Rivers for $295 (up to $55,000). Rocky Ridge did not give a price. Mile High's coverage is per container per month.
- Delivery timing is not the same. Great Plains guarantees April 22 with a late payment. Buckeye gives a 3-day window. Rocky Ridge (5 to 9 days), Two Rivers (7 to 12 days) and Mile High (5 to 8 business days) count from pickup, so the dates depend on which pickup day you get.
- Dedicated truck or shared. Buckeye says your things are the only load. Rocky Ridge says your shipment may share a truck. The others do not say.
- Denver access is still unknown. Long carry, shuttle and stair fees from four companies depend on whether a truck can get close to the Denver building and how many flights there are. Until you know, the possible extra fees could change the order.
- The quotes expire at different times. Mile High's price is held only 7 days from October 16, 2026. Great Plains is valid until November 15, 2026, and Buckeye until November 30, 2026. Rocky Ridge and Two Rivers do not say.
