**1. The main idea**

The water on Earth keeps moving in a loop between the ground and the sky. It doesn't get used up. The total amount stays about the same. It just changes form (ice, liquid water, or an invisible gas called water vapor) and moves to new places.

The sun is the engine. Its heat turns liquid water into vapor that rises into the air. Up high, the air is cooler, so the vapor turns back into tiny drops that form clouds. When the drops get heavy enough, gravity pulls them down as rain or snow. Then the water soaks into the ground or runs off into rivers and oceans, and the loop starts again.

**2. Condensation vs. precipitation**

They're two steps in a row, not the same thing. Condensation happens up in the air: water vapor cools and turns into tiny droplets, and lots of those droplets together make a cloud. Nothing is falling yet. Precipitation is the next step: the droplets join together and get too heavy to float, so they fall as rain, snow, sleet or hail. A quick way to remember it: condensation makes the cloud, precipitation empties it.

**3. A worked example**

*You take a hot shower, and afterward the bathroom mirror is foggy and dripping.*

Step 1: The hot water heats the air and some of it becomes water vapor. That's evaporation.
Step 2: The vapor touches the cool mirror and cools down.
Step 3: It turns into tiny drops of liquid water on the glass. That's condensation. The fog on the mirror is like a mini cloud.
Step 4: The tiny drops join into bigger drops, get heavy, and run down the mirror. That's like precipitation: gravity pulling the water down.
